**Migration.** Four establishments were published as a French-only record and an Arabic-only record, and are now one bilingual record each. The record keeps the id its French half shipped under, and the other id is retired for good: `01-eph-06` (the EPH of Adrar, merged into `01-eph-01`), `14-eph-07` (the EPH of Sougueur, into `14-eph-04`), `31-chu-04` (the CHU of Oran, into `31-chu-01`) and `31-chu-03` (the EHU of Oran, into `31-chu-02`). If you hold one of them, `data/retired-ids.json` carries a `migrations` entry naming the id it was merged into, both Ministry of Health post ids, and the evidence the pair rests on, so an old join key can be followed rather than only found missing. The record count falls from 661 to 657 and nothing was deleted.

**Why a hand-written pair table.** The registry publishes every establishment twice, once in French and once in Arabic, under two post ids, and the previous releases recovered those pairs from post adjacency plus a consonant-skeleton name check. Adjacency reaches at most two post ids, because only 42.9% of French posts have an Arabic post at id+1 and a wider window pairs the wrong facilities more often than the right ones. These four entered their halves five to fourteen ids apart, and three of them sit in a city whose establishments name nothing but the city, so no automatic rule can tell one facility's halves from the next one's. Widening the window would have decided them on the registry's own class vocabulary, which every establishment in the registry carries.

So they are asserted by hand in [`packages/sante/scripts/twin-pair-table.mjs`](https://github.com/yasserstudio/geoalgeria/blob/main/packages/sante/scripts/twin-pair-table.mjs), and **every row cites a second source, never the registry**: the OpenStreetMap elements [`node/5477990321`](https://www.openstreetmap.org/node/5477990321) and [`node/2947184170`](https://www.openstreetmap.org/node/2947184170) for Sougueur, each naming the one public hospital of the commune in both scripts; [`relation/5484301`](https://www.openstreetmap.org/relation/5484301) and Wikidata [Q7894776](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q7894776) for the CHU of Oran, agreeing on both names independently; [`way/71816173`](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/71816173) for the EHU at USTO, a ministry-run university hospital at a different site from the CHU; and [`way/393042095`](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/393042095), whose `official_name` reads "Etablissement Public Hospitalier Ibn Sina Adrar", for the EPH of Adrar. Each row also records the registry titles it was written about, and the build stops if the Ministry renames, moves or withdraws one of them, so a pair can never outlive the reading behind it. Google is not a source here, nor anywhere in this repository.

**Four cases are deliberately left as two records.** Skikda and Ouargla each publish an "Etablissement Hospitalier" and an "Etablissement Public Hospitalier" of the city, in both languages, and nothing outside the registry names either one by its legal kind in both scripts. Ouargla carries a second doubt on top of the first: only one general hospital of the city could be found in any source, so it is not even established that those four posts are two establishments rather than one entered under an older and a newer legal name. Pairing them would settle that by assumption. All four are listed in `unpaired_candidates` in the report, with the reading and the date behind the refusal.

**An Arabic locative particle was reading as a place's own name.** `بأدرار` and `بادرار`, "at Adrar", transliterate to `badrar`, which the commune-name exclusion did not hold, so the bare city name survived as if it told two establishments of Adrar apart. It had stamped the OpenStreetMap EPSP of Adrar, [`way/433204559`](https://www.openstreetmap.org/way/433204559), onto the Arabic half-record of the EPH of Adrar. A token that is a commune or wilaya name reached through a leading locative particle is now the place name it is, and the EPH of Adrar publishes the reviewed Wikidata coordinate it was pinned to.

Also: bilingual records rise from 590 to 594, geocoded fall from 591 to 587 of a smaller total with the same 70 establishments unplaced, and exact points go from 133 to 132, the one lost being that wrong EPSP stamp. Every decision, the evidence for each hand-asserted pair and every case left unpaired is in [`quality/sante-twin-recovery.json`](https://github.com/yasserstudio/geoalgeria/blob/main/quality/sante-twin-recovery.json), regenerated on every build so it cannot drift from the data. Both retirement sites, the twin merge and the repeat merge, now forward through one shared helper, so a retired id always resolves to a live record in a single hop even when its target is later absorbed by another merge.
